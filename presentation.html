CRM Data Governance Case Study

Turning CRM data quality issues into an operations plan

This project audits contact-record quality, identifies governance risks, and turns the findings into a practical cleanup roadmap. It demonstrates CRM administration, data governance, customer lifecycle thinking, and operational problem solving.

Business Problem

CRM records were usable, but not governed consistently

The risk was not simply "messy data." Missing emails, incomplete addresses, placeholder values, and inconsistent fields could reduce contactability, weaken reporting reliability, and make follow-up dependent on individual memory instead of clean CRM process.

The project focused on a practical operations question: which problems create the highest customer-contact risk, which issues affect reporting, and what should be fixed first?

Investigation

Initial data audit

Questions were asked in plain business language, then translated into record counts for missing phone numbers, emails, and addresses.

Prioritization

Cleanup priorities were based on contactability risk

The largest cleanup opportunity was email coverage, while the highest-severity issue was a small set of records with no usable contact fields. The dashboard separated broad cleanup volume from urgent contactability risk.

  • 811 records missing email coverage.
  • 407 records missing address but still contactable.
  • 153 records with only mobile among core contact fields.

Prioritization logic: start with records most likely to block customer follow-up, then clean high-volume fields that improve segmentation, reporting, and future campaign readiness.

Root Cause Analysis

The portfolio was active; governance was the problem

A stale-record theory did not hold. The audit confirmed there were no records older than 12 months by last modified date. That changed the recommendation: the focus should not be archive cleanup first. It should be governance, standardization, and better data-entry controls.

Governance Findings

Field design and property use created reporting risk

This is the central finding: the CRM needed governance rules, not just one-time cleanup.

Duplicate Fields

Similar properties created uncertainty about which field should be trusted for reporting or workflow triggers.

Placeholder Values

Invalid placeholders made some records appear more complete than they were, weakening data-quality confidence.

Job Title Misuse

Non-standard values in job-title fields created normalization issues and made segmentation unreliable.

Remediation Plan

Prevent, standardize, consolidate

Prevent

Set required fields, validation rules, and entry guidance so the same data problems do not keep entering the CRM.

Standardize

Define allowed values, naming conventions, and ownership expectations for contact fields used in reporting.

Consolidate

Merge or retire duplicate fields so teams know which property is the source of truth.

30-60-90 Plan

How I would operationalize the fix

30

Stop bad data at entry

Document field definitions, identify source-of-truth properties, add required-field expectations, and prioritize records with the highest contactability risk.

60

Clean high-impact records

Resolve missing email/address gaps where possible, normalize placeholder values, and train coworkers on the updated HubSpot process.

90

Govern and monitor

Consolidate duplicate fields, create recurring data-quality checks, and track CRM hygiene as an operations metric.
