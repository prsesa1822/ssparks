1. Business Problem

CRM records were usable, but not governed consistently

The risk was not simply "messy data." Missing emails, incomplete addresses, placeholder values, duplicate fields, and inconsistent field usage could reduce contactability, weaken reporting reliability, and make follow-up dependent on individual memory instead of clean CRM process.

The operating question was: which data problems create customer-contact risk, which problems create reporting risk, and what should be fixed first to protect follow-up quality?

2. Investigation

Initial data audit

Plain-language questions were converted into CRM record counts for missing phone numbers, emails, and addresses.

3. Prioritization

Contactability risk came first

Broadest cleanup need

811 records were missing email coverage, limiting follow-up options and segmentation quality.

Still contactable

407 records were missing addresses but still had another usable contact path.

Highest severity

153 mobile-only records represented a thinner contactability profile and deserved focused review.

4. Root Cause Analysis

The portfolio was active; governance was the problem

A stale-record assumption did not hold. The audit found no records older than 12 months by last modified date. That changed the remediation strategy: the priority was not archive cleanup first; it was governance, standardization, field rules, and better data-entry habits.

5. Governance Findings

Field design and property use created reporting risk

This is the central finding: the CRM needed governance rules, not just one-time cleanup.

Duplicate fields

Similar properties created uncertainty about which field should be trusted for reporting, workflows, or leadership visibility.

Placeholder values

Invalid placeholders made some records look more complete than they really were, weakening data-quality confidence.

Normalization issues

Job title and birthday usage patterns pointed to inconsistent field behavior and segmentation risk.

6. Recommendations

Prevent, standardize, consolidate

Prevent

Set required fields, validation rules, intake guidance, and record-owner expectations so bad data is stopped at entry.

Standardize

Define allowed values, field definitions, naming conventions, and quality checks for fields used in reporting.

Consolidate

Merge, retire, or document duplicate properties so teams know which field is the source of truth.

7. Business Impact

What this would improve for a customer operations team

More reliable follow-up

Cleaner contact fields reduce missed outreach risk and make customer lifecycle management less dependent on individual memory.

Better reporting trust

Standardized fields and clear source-of-truth rules improve the credibility of dashboards, segmentation, and leadership reporting.

Scalable CRM habits

A 30-60-90 governance plan turns cleanup into an operating rhythm through training, validation, and recurring quality review.
